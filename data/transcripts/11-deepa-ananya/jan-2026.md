Deepa: Ananya, I'm excited about this quarter. The enterprise dashboard redesign is a big opportunity for us.

Ananya: I know, right? I've been thinking about it non-stop since we talked about this last week. This could be the thing that differentiates us from Lattice and Engagedly and really sets us apart.

Deepa: Exactly. So let's talk about your Q1 goals. You have the enterprise dashboard UX redesign as the main deliverable. We're targeting a May 15 launch. Does that timeline work for you?

Ananya: Okay, so that's about 4.5 months. That feels doable actually. What's the full scope of what we're redesigning?

Deepa: Full redesign of the dashboard experience. Currently it's very table-heavy, very dense information dump. We want to transform it to be more visual, more intuitive, easier to scan. Widgets, customizable layouts, maybe some interactive elements.

Ananya: I've been looking at what Slack and Notion are doing with their dashboards. Very widget-based, lots of user customization options. Clean interaction patterns. I think we could do something elegant and modern like that.

Ananya: I also looked at Apple's widgets and how they handle density and complexity. There's something really nice about how they let users choose what they see without overwhelming them.

Deepa: I love that direction you're thinking in. So widget-based customizable dashboard is the concept. Did your user research findings support that approach?

Ananya: Yeah, actually in the research we did last month with power users, they kept saying they wanted to see "what matters to me at a glance." They didn't want to have to scroll through a long table. Widget-based customization is perfect for that need.

Ananya: We also asked about what features matter most in their current dashboards. Did you see the research summary?

Deepa: I did. The top 3 were real-time data, customizable views, and performance metrics. That all fits with the widget approach.

Ananya: Right. So we're designing based on actual user feedback, not just what looks pretty.

Deepa: Exactly. That's the best approach.

Deepa: One thing I want to make sure of — are there any existing constraints we need to design within? Like technical limitations or brand guidelines?

Ananya: Good question. I assume we need to stick to our existing color palette and typography system?

Deepa: Yeah, that's right. But this might be a good opportunity to evolve them slightly if it makes sense for the new direction. Let's decide on that as we see your concepts.

Ananya: Makes sense. I'll keep the core brand but think about how to make it feel more modern and refined.

Deepa: And what about the competitive landscape? What are Lattice and Engagedly doing?

Ananya: Lattice has a pretty basic dashboard, honestly just report tables and charts. Nothing special. Engagedly went more visual with colors and icons but honestly it feels cluttered to me. Like, there's too much visual noise. I think there's a real opportunity for us to do elegant simplicity — visual but calm.

Ananya: I was also looking at Workday and SAP SuccessFactors. They're more enterprise-focused and heavy. That's definitely what we don't want to be. We want lightweight and modern.

Deepa: Good research. I totally agree with your direction. Elegant simplicity is where the market opportunity is.

Deepa: So the direction is clear in my mind. Let me ask you though — what does success look like for you personally? How will you know the redesign is actually good?

Ananya: I think if we get good feedback from the product team and from real users in testing, that would indicate success. And honestly, if it feels beautiful, you know? Like, if I'm proud of it and wouldn't be embarrassed to put it on my portfolio.

Deepa: Yes. Beauty definitely matters. But I also want to ground it in usability metrics. Let's say we target a SUS score of 78 for the new dashboard, compared to the current dashboard which scores 68?

Ananya: What's SUS exactly? I've heard of it but I don't know the details.

Deepa: System Usability Scale. It's a 10-question survey that measures perceived usability. Ranges from 0 to 100. Our current dashboard scores 68, which is okay but not great — that's barely above average. We want to push that to 78, which would be genuinely good.

Ananya: Okay, so usability is part of the goal too. That's important context. I want to make sure I'm designing for real user needs, not just aesthetics.

Deepa: Exactly. Beauty and usability working together. So that's goal one — dashboard redesign with SUS score of 78.

Ananya: Got it. What's goal two?

Deepa: Goal two is the design system components. We're at 6 components in our design system right now. We want to get to 10 by end of Q1. These are reusable pieces — buttons, cards, modals, input fields, etc.

Ananya: 10 components total. So 4 new ones in the next 3 months alongside the dashboard?

Deepa: Right. That's in parallel to the dashboard work. Not a huge lift per component, but it adds up and really strengthens our system.

Ananya: I can do that. I was already thinking we need a better card component for the dashboard anyway. That could be component number one.

Deepa: Great thinking. So dashboard redesign, SUS 78, and 4 new components. What's your timeline thinking for all this?
